Feature 07 / history of the game

From peach baskets to positionless basketball

Tap any milestone to reveal how it changed the way basketball is played. The game’s strategy did not appear all at once—it evolved whenever rules, leagues, and ideas changed.

1891

Naismith invents “basket ball”

James Naismith creates an indoor winter game in Springfield, Massachusetts and writes 13 original rules.

Strategic impact: The first version emphasized passing and placement. Dribbling, the shot clock, and the three-point line were not part of the original game.
1936

Basketball becomes an Olympic medal sport

Men’s basketball is formally contested at the Berlin Games; the first final is played outdoors in rain.

Strategic impact: Olympic competition helps basketball become an international game, eventually producing different playing styles and systems around the world.
1946

The BAA begins

The Basketball Association of America launches; it later merges with the NBL and becomes the NBA in 1949.

Strategic impact: A stable major professional league creates a stage for specialized coaching, star players, and rule innovation.
1954

The 24-second shot clock arrives

NBA teams can no longer hold the ball indefinitely; a possession must produce a shot quickly.

Strategic impact: Pace increases. Teams need actions that create a good shot before time expires—the same decision problem studied in this app.
1979

The NBA adopts the three-point line

A long-distance make is now worth three points instead of two.

Strategic impact: Shot value changes. A 3 at 34% produces 1.02 expected points, slightly more than a 2 at 50% (1.00)—the mathematical heart of Shot Lab.
1997

The WNBA tips off

The league’s inaugural season begins, expanding the highest level of professional women’s basketball in the United States.

Strategic impact: New teams, stars, coaches, and audiences add to the development and global reach of the sport.
2010s

The spacing and analytics era

Teams increasingly measure shot value and build offenses around the rim, free throws, and three-pointers.

Strategic impact: The midrange shot becomes more selective, floor spacing grows, and players at every position are asked to pass, dribble, and shoot.
Rule-change analyzer

Why the three changed everything

A three-pointer is worth 1.5 times a two. Therefore, its break-even accuracy against a 50% two is only 33.3%.

50% × 2 = 1.00
33.3% × 3 ≈ 1.00
